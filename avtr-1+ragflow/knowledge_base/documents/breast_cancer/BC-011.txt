Inherited risk and genetic counseling

[DOC_ID: BC-011]
Inherited harmful variants in genes such as BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, and several others can increase breast cancer risk. Features that may prompt a hereditary-risk assessment include a strong family history, breast cancer at a young age, ovarian or pancreatic cancer in the family, male breast cancer, or a known familial variant. Family history on both the mother’s and father’s sides matters.

Genetic counseling helps a person understand whether testing may be appropriate, possible results, limitations, effects on relatives, and options after testing. A direct-to-consumer result or a family story should not be treated as a clinical diagnosis. Testing decisions and risk-reduction options should be discussed with a qualified genetics professional and clinician.
