Follow-up care and survivorship

[DOC_ID: BC-012]
After treatment, follow-up is individualized. A survivorship care plan can summarize treatment received, possible late effects, recommended follow-up, and which clinician is responsible for each part of care. Follow-up may include physical or clinical breast examinations and appropriate breast imaging. A reconstructed breast after mastectomy is not typically screened with routine mammography, while the remaining natural breast usually continues to receive recommended imaging.

Treatment effects can include fatigue, lymphedema, fertility concerns, emotional distress, body-image or sexual-health changes, and late effects that emerge months or years later. The health care team can help prevent, monitor, or manage these effects. New persistent symptoms or concerns about recurrence should be reported rather than self-diagnosed. Emotional, caregiver, rehabilitation, and financial support are legitimate parts of cancer care.
