Biomarkers and breast cancer subtypes

[DOC_ID: BC-007]
After diagnosis, tumor tissue is commonly tested for estrogen receptors (ER), progesterone receptors (PR), and HER2. These biomarkers help estimate tumor behavior and identify treatments that may work. Hormone-receptor-positive cancer may respond to endocrine or hormone therapy. HER2-positive cancer may respond to medicines that target HER2.

Triple-negative breast cancer lacks ER, PR, and HER2 expression. It is generally treated using approaches other than hormone therapy or HER2-targeted therapy, such as chemotherapy and, in some situations, immunotherapy. Ki-67 is another marker of cell division that may contribute information. Biomarker status can sometimes change if cancer returns, so clinicians may recommend retesting. A biomarker result must not be used by the assistant to select a drug or dose for an individual.
