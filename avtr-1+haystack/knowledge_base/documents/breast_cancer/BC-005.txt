High-risk screening and breast awareness

[DOC_ID: BC-005]
People at higher-than-average risk may need a screening plan different from the average-risk schedule. Breast MRI is sometimes used together with mammography for people at high risk. MRI is not normally used by itself for average-risk screening because it can appear abnormal when cancer is absent. Dense breasts can both increase risk and make tumors harder to see on a mammogram.

Breast self-awareness means knowing how one’s breasts normally look and feel and reporting a new change. It is not a substitute for recommended screening. A clinical breast exam or breast self-exam has not by itself been shown to reduce the risk of dying from breast cancer. The appropriate combination of mammography, MRI, or other assessment must be selected by a clinician using the person’s risk and history.
