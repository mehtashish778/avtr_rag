Breast cancer overview

[DOC_ID: BC-001]
Breast cancer begins when abnormal cells in breast tissue grow without normal control. It commonly starts in milk ducts or milk-producing lobules. In situ disease is confined to where it began; invasive cancer has entered nearby breast tissue. Cancer can spread through lymph nodes or blood to distant organs, which is called metastatic breast cancer.

Breast cancer occurs mainly in women, but men can also develop it. WHO estimates that about 0.5% to 1% of breast cancers occur in men. Early detection linked to timely diagnosis and appropriate treatment improves outcomes. General information cannot determine whether an individual has cancer; symptoms or abnormal screening results require assessment by a qualified clinician.
