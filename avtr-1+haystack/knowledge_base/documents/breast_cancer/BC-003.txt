Risk factors and risk reduction

[DOC_ID: BC-003]
Breast cancer risk reflects a combination of factors. Risk generally increases with age. Other established factors include inherited harmful variants such as BRCA1 or BRCA2, dense breasts, a personal history of breast cancer or certain high-risk breast conditions, relevant family history, and previous chest radiation at a young age.

Potentially modifiable factors associated with higher risk include physical inactivity, overweight or obesity after menopause, drinking alcohol, and some forms of menopausal hormone therapy. Having a risk factor does not mean that cancer will occur, and people without a known family history can still develop breast cancer. Healthy behavior may reduce risk but cannot guarantee prevention. Individual risk and screening decisions should be discussed with a clinician.
